The choice gets made early, by whoever writes the first schema. So this is who the role has to reach.

Who

CTOs, founding engineers and engineering heads. They pick the database.

Which companies

Seed to Series A startups in India, the stage MongoDB for Startups is built for, building AI or data products.

When

In the months after a round, when they hire the first engineers and pick, or rebuild, the stack.

What they want

To ship fast now without a rebuild at Series B, and cloud bills that don't jump as they grow.

Who they listen to

CTOs a few years ahead of them, their investors and their own engineers. Rarely a vendor.

How I'd approach the role

Reach the engineers who pick the database, through people they already trust.

  1. 1

    Invite the CTOs and founding engineers right after they raise, through the funds that backed them, while the stack is still open.

    timing
  2. 2

    Let builders do the convincing. A CTO who scaled on MongoDB is far more believable to a founder than anything we say about ourselves.

    trust
  3. 3

    Keep every room small and picked by hand. Twenty CTOs who are choosing a stack are worth more than two hundred registrations.

    quality
  4. 4

    Treat every room as research. What founders tell us goes to product and Sales, and decides what the next room is about.

    learning

Two plays I'd run first

One room I've run before, and a loop that fills the next.

Play 1 · every quarter

The CTO roundtable

Founders and CTOs who just raised, around one hard problem. No slides, no pitch.

I ran these every quarter at OLake, with 20 to 25 leaders at each.

Play 2 · runs alongside

The story loop

Every room gives us a story, and every story brings in the next founder.

  • 1FindI find a founder or CTO who scaled on MongoDB, like the team at Emergent, and ask what they'd choose again
  • 2WriteI sit with them, push them to tell it in their own words and help shape it. It goes out under their name
  • 3ShareThrough fund newsletters, founder communities and their own channels. Sales gets it too, as proof from a peer
  • 4NextFounders who read it reach out. The ones who build well become the next story

At OLake I coordinated our six customer stories and pushed each team to tell it in their own words. PhysicsWallah's went out under their own engineer's name.

Fix the biggest drop, then make it repeatable

  • Find where founders fall away: getting into the room, applying, or starting to build. Fix that one first.
  • Send what founders said to product and Builder Relations, and startups ready for a bigger conversation to Sales.
  • Use the first published story to fill next quarter's first room.
  • Agree a quarterly calendar with three to five funds and accelerators.

How I'm thinking about itFixing one drop properly does more than tuning all three a little.
