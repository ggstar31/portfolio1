The Full Story

The "Why" behind the execution

A 2-minute read. Two products, one company, one lesson.

01

Datazip, a unified data stack platform for enterprises

As the first business hire I built the commercial machinery that didn't exist: ICP and primary research, sales enablement, case studies, outreach across email and social, launches on Product Hunt, Hacker News and Reddit. Revenue moved from $100K to $500K ARR. We learnt as we moved further about the limited scalability, without keeping defending the current product, we moved faster with the honest read of the market.

02

Finding the real problem

We went looking for the real problem. Events and dozens of conversations pointed at it: mid-size companies still found data replication painful, slow and overpriced. That's how OLake was born, and why we built it in the open: open source was the cheapest way to learn if developers actually wanted it before betting the company.

03

OLake, an open source data replication tool

It meant a completely different buyer, and this is where first principles earned their keep. Developers resist being sold to for two rational reasons: switching costs (they run an existing stack, and migration is real work) and the builder's instinct (anything that looks simple, they'd rather write themselves). Ads don't move either. What moves developers is benchmarks, technical content, and advocates they already trust talking about a pain they already have.

04

Education, not promotion

So the playbook was education, not promotion, a system I'd first learned running webinars at Mudrex: create evangelists before you sell. We hosted sessions once or twice a month, and I convinced industry leaders to walk audiences through the stack itself, not our pitch. Session learnings became technical documentation and blogs; blogs became social distribution; attendees became a community.

05

The engine started feeding itself

100+ user and developer conversations turned into product requirements and contributed to closing the $1M pre-seed. I embedded with engineering on two launches, scoped requirements with the engineers directly, wired AI that cut execution time about 75% across 8+ channels, and ran the whole pre-sales cycle alone, demo to pre-deployment. Then the engine started feeding itself. More than 5% of event attendees converted to design partners. Zero to 1,500+ GitHub stars, 45 contributors, 700+ Slack members; LinkedIn from 0 to 18K. The events went from local meetups to global rooms, San Francisco included, which did something subtler than pipeline: it gave Indian developers a channel to contribute to something with global reach. In 8 months: 6K+ MQLs, 55+ POC conversions, 30+ clients in production, 4:1 LTV:CAC on enterprise.
