Biggest Learning

In categories where the user risks money, empathy is not a soft skill, it's the research method.

The equilibrium between what people want and what you supply can only be found in person.

The Full Story

The "Why" behind the execution

A 2-minute read. Trust deficit, research, rebrand, zero to one.

The product was a digital P2P savings and liquidity platform, NBFC-P2P regulated, for households most banks overlook: informal income, thin credit files, and every reason to distrust an app that asks for money.

In this market the trust deficit isn't a marketing problem sitting next to the product. It is the product.

And it cannot be advertised away, because for an audience that has been burned, every pitch that sounds like a pitch confirms the suspicion.

First principle

When you can't buy belief, go find out what people actually need and build the story from there.

75+ user interviews, Tier 2 cities

I travelled across Tier 2 cities and ran 75+ user interviews. The gap was consistent: fintechs design for the user they imagine, not the one standing in front of them. Those interviews became the ground truth for everything that followed.

Rebranding

Design is the first trust signal a user ever sees. I led the rebrand end to end, UI and UX for site and app, and rebuilt the product narrative around what the interviews said people needed to hear before they'd deposit a single rupee.

The zero to one

Then the zero-to-one: Vrddi, the B2B product. I designed the product and website, built the pitch decks, and ran the launch. Two key clients signed in the first month; 200+ users, 5 enterprise clients and 2 partnership arms inside two. The same research-first collateral thinking travelled well: brochures I designed pulled 100+ inbound leads at fintech events across Singapore, UAE and India, working rooms I wasn't even in.

What compounded

Capital pooled grew from $30M to $48M+. Recognition followed the numbers rather than preceding them, which is the correct order.
